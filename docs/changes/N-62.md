title: Transitions spanning the empty blue half of a symmetric line integrate across it with the red half mirrored

A symmetric line A (no `ASYMM`, static unpolarized atmosphere, one component) has wavelengths on its red half only, and since N-55 no other wavelength falls in its blue half. A transition T whose range spans A then has an interval [λ_b, λ₀] in its merged grid, λ_b the first point blueward of A's blue half, and the trapezoid (`getwlambda_cont`, `getwlambda_line`, `getwlambda_mrt`) took J linear across it: across the whole blue half of A's absorption profile. With the Ca II continua across the Lyman lines that put the Ca II populations off by up to 40 % (H_20).

**The fix.** `mirrorQuadrature` (`getlambda.c`), called at the end of `SortLambda`, integrates that interval with A's red half mirrored, J(λ₀ − d) = J(λ₀ + d), the assumption that makes A symmetric in the first place: a node at v = 2λ₀ − λ_r for every grid point λ_r of A's red half, then the trapezoid from the last of them to λ_b. The weight of node v goes to point r, where J is known, scaled by the ratio of T's integrand without J at v and at λ_r:
- continuum: (α(v)/v)/(α_r/λ_r), α linear across [λ_b, λ₀];
- line or molecular line: ((λ_r − λ₀,T)/(v − λ₀,T))². T has no points in A's blue half, so A lies in T's far wing, where the profile goes as 1/Δλ² at every depth.

The weights go to the new field `wquad` of the transitions concerned; all others keep the trapezoid, bit for bit. The upward and downward rates use the same weights, so detailed balance holds node by node, and the line profile normalization (`wphi`) uses them too. No wavelengths are added and no formal solutions; the iteration counts of the runs below are unchanged. One message gives the number of transitions and (transition, line) pairs.

**Measured** (FAL-C, static, ITER_LIMIT 10⁻⁶; reference: every line asymmetric; largest |Δn/n| in levels holding more than 10⁻³ of the atom; evidence `claude/RH/evidence/t29-n62`):

| model | pairs | Ca, before | Ca, after | H, before | H, after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H_20 + Ca II | 692 | 0.40 | 1.7×10⁻³ (Ca III; Ca II 7.7×10⁻⁴) | 5.5×10⁻³ | 4.4×10⁻³ |
| H_6 + Ca II | 19 | 1.1×10⁻² | 4.3×10⁻⁴ | 1.1×10⁻³ | 2.7×10⁻⁵ |
| H_6 + Ca II + Mg II | 64 | 2.4×10⁻³ | 9.6×10⁻⁵ | 3.3×10⁻³ | 2.9×10⁻⁵ |
| H_CRD + Ca II | 49 | 6.5×10⁻⁵ | 1.4×10⁻⁶ | 1.5×10⁻³ | 1.4×10⁻⁵ |
| Ca II alone | 0 | 0 | 0 | | |

Mg II in H_6 + Ca II + Mg II: 2.2×10⁻³ → 1.5×10⁻⁵. Making every line asymmetric instead costs +19 % to +69 % wavelengths.

**Not fixed here.** What remains is the symmetric fold itself, where J is not symmetric about λ₀. The largest cases: H9 in H_20 (4.3×10⁻³ in H), whose blue half holds a line of the Kurucz list that the grid never samples (N-64 is to warn about such lines), and Lyα in H_20 (1.6×10⁻³ in Ca III; `ASYMM` in the atom file removes it). The PRD scattering integral (`scatter.c`) still interpolates J linearly across such a gap inside a PRD line's range (Ca II K across H8 with H_20 and H_CRD).

**Results.** Change for every input with a transition that spans a symmetric line. `run_tests.py` against `modernize` c34ae0a (tolerance mode): 58 of 60 checks pass; `1d/twoactive` and `sph/small` change, by 5.9×10⁻⁴ and 1.8×10⁻³ in I, largest in the Ca II K core (these cases stop after 4 iterations, so the size says little about converged results). Thread counts and the sanitizer runs agree.

Tests: new unit test `test_mirror`. For a J symmetric about the centre of the line, the mirrored weights on the merged grid reproduce the trapezoid on the full grid to rounding: a continuum (α linear) and a line with the symmetric line in its far wing. It also checks that the weights add up to the length of the range (also for a continuum that ends inside the red half), that the symmetric line itself and a transition starting at its centre keep the trapezoid, and that a second call gives the same weights.
