title: Hydrostatic equilibrium counts each H2 molecule as two hydrogen nuclei

`Hydrostatic` (`rhf1d/hydrostat.c`, `HYDROSTATIC = TRUE`, `SOLVER = NG` only) solves per depth point for n_H, n_e, n_p, n_H2 and nHtot. Its row for hydrogen number conservation was n_H + n_p + n_H2 = nHtot: each H2 molecule counted as one hydrogen nucleus. The chemical equilibrium (`chemequil.c`) counts two, and also the hydrogen in H- and in the other molecules. The two partitions of the same nHtot disagree by n_H2. Two smaller inconsistencies sat in the same rows:
- The H2 equilibrium constant was taken as n_H2 / (n_H + n_p)^2, while the row uses neutral hydrogen, n_H2 = Phi_H2 n_H^2 (as in the chemical equilibrium). This is off by 2 n_p/n_H, about 2e-4 where H2 forms.
- The hydrogen in H- and in other molecules was not counted.

The row is now n_H + n_p + 2 n_H2 = fH nHtot, with Phi_H2 = n_H2 / n_H^2. fH = (H->ntotal + 2 n_H2) / nHtot is the fraction of nHtot that the chemical equilibrium leaves in atoms, protons and H2. It is held fixed during the hydrostatic iteration, as F holds the electrons of the metals. A state from the chemical equilibrium is then an exact starting point: no residual from the partition.

What it did: the iteration does not stall. The chemical equilibrium in Background() sets H->ntotal = nHtot - 2 n_H2. The hydrostatic step then sets it back to nHtot - n_H2 and rescales the H populations. Both happen before the population update, so the population changes per iteration don't see it, and runs converge as before (FAL-C, `SOLVE_NE = ITERATION`: max change for H 5.9e-7 after 400 iterations before, 6.4e-7 after). It converges to the wrong atmosphere. The pressure is balanced with n_H2 hydrogen nuclei missing from nHtot, so the converged nHtot is too low by n_H2 / (1 + beta) (beta: the metals and the turbulent pressure, about 0.1). So are the metal densities, abundance x nHtot.

The "6.3e-4 floor for H" seen in the N-16 study is not this bug's effect. It was the measure sum(H populations) / nHtot - 1, which is n_H2/nHtot by construction because H->ntotal excludes molecules. The e2e test `hydrostatic_final` now checks every depth point, not only those without H2. It counts the hydrogen nuclei in the atoms and protons, in H- and in every molecule of molecules.out (2 per H2; new reader `rhtest.output.read_molecules`, which replaces `read_first_molecule`). Its converged case is off by 6.3e-4 before the fix, by 1.1e-6 with H2 counted twice but without fH, and by 7e-8 with the fix; the tolerance is 5e-7.

Measured (evidence: `/hao/solar1/dwijn/claude/RH/evidence/t12-n44/`, README and summary.txt), H_6 and Ca II active:
- FAL-C (n_H2/nHtot up to 6.2e-4 at 5150 K): nHtot +5.6e-4 at most. Emergent intensity up to 2.6e-3 in the UV continua (110-165 nm), median 1.1e-4, Ca II H and K cores 1e-4.
- T5750_g4.5: nHtot +5.5e-4 at most.
- FAL-C with T x 0.7 (a test model, T_min 3080 K, n_H2/nHtot up to 6 %): nHtot +5.5 %, Ca II H and K up to 2.3 %. The error scales with n_H2/nHtot, so it is large in cool stars.
- Including the hydrogen in H- and other molecules (fH) changes nHtot by at most 1.6e-5 (cool model) and the FAL-C spectrum by at most 4e-6 beyond counting H2 twice.

Results change only for `HYDROSTATIC = TRUE` runs; `run_tests.sh --exact` (no hydrostatic case) is byte-identical. Same code upstream.
