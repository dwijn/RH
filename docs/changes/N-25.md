title: Polarized Bezier solvers no longer depend on the reference direction of Q and U (changes results)

The DELO-Bezier solvers (1-D, 2-D, 3-D) limit the derivatives of the source vector and of the propagation matrix with Steffen's limiter, which is nonlinear: zero at an extremum, otherwise the centred slope clipped to twice the smaller one-sided slope. Applied to Q and U, or to eta_Q and eta_U, one at a time, it does not commute with turning the reference frame, which mixes them: the result depended on the arbitrary reference direction of linear polarization. The pairs that turn with the frame, (S_Q, S_U), (eta_Q, eta_U) and (rho_Q, rho_U), are now limited as vectors: the derivative is zero where the one-sided slopes point in opposite directions (negative dot product), and otherwise the centred slope shortened to at most twice the shorter one-sided slope. This uses only lengths and a dot product, so it turns with the frame, and it is Steffen's limiter for a single component. For a vertical ray on FAL-C with a depth-dependent field, turning the field azimuth by 35 degrees now turns (Q, U) to 3e-14 (before: off by 7e-3 of their maximum, V by 2e-4, I by 1e-5); on a grey atmosphere to 1e-14 at every depth resolution (before 4.8e-3 at 40 points to 5.0e-6 at 320). Results change only where the field azimuth varies along the ray: with a fixed azimuth (Q, U) keeps its direction and the vector limiter gives the same derivatives as before (the regression cases agree to 1e-17). The e2e test now requires exact rotation on FAL-C and the grey atmosphere, and a unit test checks that the limiter commutes with rotations on random data and reduces to Steffen's for one component.
