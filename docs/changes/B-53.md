title: 3-D long characteristics cross the vertical planes at the right column or row for rays with a negative direction cosine

`setStencil` (`rhsc3d/geometry.c`) takes the plane index of a long-characteristic crossing as `round(y / dy)` for an XZ plane and `round(x / dx)` for a YZ plane. RH defined its own `round`, `modf(x + 0.5)`, which truncates toward zero and so returns j + 1 for a negative integer j: on rays with a negative μx or μy every crossing read S and χ from the neighbouring plane. The function also replaced libm's `round` for the whole `rhsc3d` program (it had no other caller). It is removed, and nothing is rounded any more: `setStencil` records the index of the plane, ±(l + 1), when it tabulates the crossing (`Intersect.index`), instead of recovering it from the floating-point position.

`test_formal_3d` now interpolates the coordinates x, y and z with every point of the downward-sweep long characteristics of five rays (all octants), with both horizontal interpolations, on a 128 × 128 mesh with unevenly spaced layers (12 to 80 points per characteristic), and requires all four oblique rays to have one: exact to 7e-16. Before, the three rays with a negative direction cosine were off by dx.

Results change in 3-D wherever long characteristics are used and the atmosphere is not exactly uniform between columns. `run_tests.sh` in tolerance mode against 1bfaa2d: 33 of 34 cases pass; 3d/small differs by 3.2e-5 in I. Its atmosphere is uniform, but its columns are not numerically identical: the first point of each row takes its upwind intensity from a long characteristic and the others interpolate it, so the populations differ slightly between columns. On the horizontally varying slab of `test_longchar_3d` (N-39, Bezier short and long characteristics, chi = 1) the emergent error drops from 0.33 to 0.25 at N = 8 and from 5.2e-2 to 4.8e-2 at N = 32.
