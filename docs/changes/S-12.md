title: ANDERSON_SAFEGUARD_WINDOW: the Anderson safeguard compares with the largest residual of the last M accepted points (default 10)

The safeguard of `SOLVER = ANDERSON` rejected an extrapolated point whose residual ‖f‖_W exceeded c = `ANDERSON_SAFEGUARD` times that of the newest accepted pair, and restarted the history. With c = 1 that assumes that G contracts in the W-norm: in the linear case |f(x_k+1)| ≤ ‖G′‖·|f(x_k)|, and slow or non-normal modes break it. The rejections then cut the history short where a slow mode needs a long one. On the `convergence_report` case (FAL-C Ca II PRD, `ERROR_LIMIT` = 10⁻⁴), where J at background-scattering wavelengths converges slowly, c = 1 rejected one extrapolated point in three. The history never grew past 2–3 columns, and the run took 260 iterations against 152 without the safeguard. Without it, though, `1d_shock_h6` does not converge.

**The change.** The new keyword `ANDERSON_SAFEGUARD_WINDOW` = M (default 10, 1 … 100) sets the reference: a trial point is rejected if ‖f‖_W > c · max ‖f_j‖_W over the M newest accepted pairs. This is a non-monotone acceptance test, in the spirit of Grippo, Lampariello & Lucidi (1986) [unverified].
- **What stays the same:** the rejection, the plain step and the restart of the history.
- **The window outlives a restart.** The norms of pairs that left the history still bound the next ones.
- **M = 1** is the old test bit for bit.
- **Output:** the start banner prints M, and the rejection line prints the reference used.
- **Input check:** readInput stops on M outside 1 … 100.

**Alternatives measured.** The experiment ran on a local branch; the numbers are in the evidence.
- **Keeping the rejected pair in the history instead of restarting (V1):** it helps as much on `shock_h6`, but costs more elsewhere: `t5750_h6` at m = 5, `3d_pert_h6_ca` +11 %, and `convergence_report` at m = 5 (338 G evaluations against 267).
- **M = 5:** about half the gain of M = 10.
- **Keep plus M = 10:** worse than either alone.

**Results.** G evaluations (rejected points included). The 1-D stress set was run with `SOLVER = ANDERSON`, c = 1 and Ng off, comparing M = 1 (before) with M = 10 (now). Only cases that differ are listed. The other 15–17 cases are identical: they have no rejections at either M.

What the numbers say:
- **`shock_h6`:** it needs half the work at every setting.
- **`t5750_h6`:** it costs 20–50 % more. There the restarts helped against a stale history; with M = 10 it has 0–2 rejections.
- **`convergence_report`** at the default depth (10): 152 iterations instead of 260 (165 G evaluations with the error estimates instead of 273), the same as with the safeguard off. At depth 5 it is 246 against 254: there the depth limits it, not the safeguard.
- **2-D and 3-D** (`ITER_LIMIT` = 10⁻⁴, depth 10):
  - `2d_pert_h6_ca_mg` is unchanged (52, no rejections).
  - `3d_pert_h6_ca` takes 57 instead of 55. A repeat run gave identical counts, so this is not run-to-run noise.

Checks:
- **Against the experiment build** on the 1-D stress set: M = 10 gives the same populations, J, spectra, iterations and rejections, bit for bit. M = 1 gives the same as the base, bit for bit.
- **`run_tests.py --exact`** against the base passes 38/38: the window changes none of its results.
- **Unit and e2e tests:** ctest passes 52/52 with gcc (`-Werror`) and with icx.

New tests:
- `test_anderson`: a trial point whose residual lies between the newest and an older accepted one is rejected with window 1 and accepted with window 3. That also holds after a restart, and the reference is that older norm exactly.
- `e2e_solver_anderson`: M = 0 stops the run at input. The forced-rejection checks of the carried blocks set M = 1.
