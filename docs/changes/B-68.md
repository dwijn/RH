title: The negative-opacity detector integrates each interval as the formal solver does, so a negative Bézier interval no longer overflows

The negative-opacity detector of N-40 (`negTauColumns`, `negtau.c`) summed trapezoidal intervals, ½(χ_k + χ_k+1) ds. The Bézier solvers integrate χ along a Bézier curve instead. Where χ changes sign inside an optically thick interval, that curve can make the interval strongly negative while the trapezoid is positive. The detector then passes the point, and `exp(−Δτ)` overflows in `Bezier3_coeffs` (SIGFPE).

**Where it showed up.** `SOLVER = NEWTON_KRYLOV` on `1d_shock_h6` died this way at a line-search trial of Newton step 28 (found in the N-3 validation). The interval was at wavelength index 141, on the most slanted ray (μ = 0.047), incoming, between depths 43 and 44:
- χ = −0.026 and +0.028 m⁻¹ over 1.9×10⁶ m of path;
- the trapezoid gives +1.3×10³;
- the solver's cubic gives −1.6×10⁴: the Steffen derivative is 0 at the extremum at 43 and steep at 44, so both control points are negative.

**The change.** `negTauIntervals` computes the optical depth of each interval with the quadrature the caller's formal solver uses (`enum negtau_quadrature`, `negtau.h`):
- **`NEGTAU_LINEAR`:** the trapezoid, as before. Used for `S_INTERPOLATION = S_LINEAR` and the spherical Feautrier solver.
- **`NEGTAU_BEZIER3_1D`:** the cubic of `bezier_1D.c`, with Steffen derivatives at the inner points and secants at the two ends. Used for `S_BEZIER3` and the polarized solver in 1-D. It reproduces the solver's value on the `shock_h6` column to 10⁻¹².
- **`NEGTAU_BEZIER_SC`:** the quadratic of the 2-D and 3-D short (and long) characteristics, with the Steffen derivative at the downwind end and the trapezoid at the end of the ray. As before, a column stands in for the ray. The quadratic depends on the direction, so both directions are taken and the smaller is kept.

The threshold (−50) and the rest of N-40 are unchanged. The shortcut that skips the check where no χ is negative still holds: both Bézier curves keep their control points between the values at the two ends (Q-34). The unit test checks this on 400 random columns with sign changes.

**Results.** Unchanged wherever the detector did not fire.
- `run_tests.py --exact` against the base passes.
- On the 1-D stress convergence set, every case that runs gives the same populations and spectra with `ANDERSON` and with `NEWTON_KRYLOV`. The one exception is `NEWTON_KRYLOV` on `1d_shock_h6`.
- That case now flags the trial at step 28 and halves λ. It runs to its budget (1500 G evaluations, 6 invalid trial points), not converged. That is the outcome N-40 recorded: `NEWTON_KRYLOV` does not converge `shock_h6`.
