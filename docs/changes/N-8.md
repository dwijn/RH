title: Check the populations of the LU fallback of statEquil; a bad solution invalidates a trial point or stops the run

`statEquil` solves the rate equations by GTH elimination, which gives non-negative populations from non-negative rates (N-31). Where a rate is negative (bad atomic data, or a negative preconditioned radiative rate in a thick line core; "negative rate 0 -> 3" in Ca II H in earlier runs) it falls back on LU, whose populations were not checked: a negative or non-finite one went into the iteration silently (into `LOG` variables as an invalid entry).

`solveRates` (`statequil.c`) now checks the populations of every location solved by LU: a population that is not finite or is below -1e-10 times the total is bad. The tolerance leaves the rounding of the LU solution of a nearly empty level alone (a relative -1e-16 to -1e-12 is no reason to stop a run); `SE_NEG_TOL`. It is relative to the total of the atom, not to the population, so the LU rounding of an ill-conditioned matrix can exceed it: a valid trial point would then be rejected (the cost is a plain step) and, outside a trial, the run would stop. No better-founded value was found; it has not triggered anywhere. `statEquil` and `statEquilMolecule` act on the first bad location of the call:

- During a trial evaluation of G (`negTauTrial(TRUE)`: an extrapolated Anderson point, a Newton-Krylov line-search trial or Jacobian probe) the trial point is invalid. `negTauInvalid` (`negtau.c`) is a second reason, beside the negative total opacity of N-40, for `negTauFound` to be true until the next `negTauReset`; `negTauMessage` returns its text; `G_eval` stops the statistical equilibrium of the remaining atoms and molecules at the first invalid one, puts the populations of `x_in` back (`stateScatter`; statEquil had overwritten them, which the formal-solution case never does) and returns FALSE (until now only after the formal solution). ANDERSON rejects the point and takes a plain step, NEWTON_KRYLOV halves lambda or zeroes the probe, exactly as for a negative opacity. `negTauInTrial` tells `statEquil` which case it is in. `negTauFound` now means that the trial point is invalid for any reason, and `negTauInvalid` outside a trial is a programming error that stops the run.
- Anywhere else the run stops with ERROR_LEVEL_2, naming atom, level (index and label), location, value and the number of such locations, and saying where it comes from: a negative population from a negative rate (the MALI preconditioning in thick line cores, or bad atomic data), a non-finite one from a non-finite rate (bad atomic data, overflow). The count is of locations solved (for a molecule, those with a non-zero density). The initial solution (`initial_xdr.c`) calls `statEquil` too and is never a trial, so a bad LU solution there stops the run with the same message. Stopping outside a trial is a change of behaviour: the run used to go on with the bad populations.

The counters and log lines of the solvers that counted rejections for a negative opacity (`Nnegtau`, renamed `Nbadtrial`: Anderson "rejected points", Newton "Jacobian probes" and "guard halvings") now speak of invalid trial points, with the two reasons (negative total opacity, N-40; negative or non-finite populations, N-8); the message of each rejection names the actual reason. No test or parser matches those lines.

Results do not change where no location falls back on LU, which is every case on modernize (no "solved with LU" line in the test cases, the stress cases or the 2-D FULL_STOKES varying-field reproducer): `run_tests.sh --exact` against the base passes. The stop and the trial rejection are exercised by the unit test only.

Tests. `test_negpop` (new): a two-level atom with a negative rate (LU population -1) and with a NaN rate: invalid in a trial with the message (atom, level, location, "negative preconditioned rates"), stop outside a trial (exit status 70, child process), nothing for non-negative rates; the invalidation is cleared by `negTauReset`.

Considered and not done: limiting Psi. The obvious cure for the negative rates themselves is a per-point limit on Psi in `addPreconditionedRates`; it was implemented and measured (branch `exp/N-8-psi-limiter`) and left out. With W = Vij wla w the two rates of a transition are, algebraically for any sign of chi, `Gamma[j][i]` (i -> j) = W (I - Psi n_j (U_j + g U_i)) (bound A) and `Gamma[i][j]` (j -> i) = W (g (2hv^3/c^2 + I) - Psi n_i (g U_i + U_j)) (bound B). Limiting by `Ieff >= 0` (Psi <= I / (n_j U_j + n_i U_i), the em bound) alone does not keep both non-negative: the downward rate goes negative at Ieff = 0 and the upward one with an inversion, so a limit needs `Psi_eff = min(Psi, A, B)` (each numerator shrunk by 1e-10 against rounding, I clipped at 0), used in Ieff and in both chi Psi U terms so that the fixed point is unchanged (Ieff + Psi_eff em = I). The em bound is then redundant for positivity: with A and B only, 55 831 of the 84 152 limited points of 1-D H + Ca II would remain (the other 28 000 have Ieff < 0 and positive rates). Measured, the limiter acts in every test case, although no summed rate goes negative anywhere on modernize: it limits the per-wavelength contributions (93 912 of 10.8 million in the 2-D FULL_STOKES reproducer, all with Ieff < 0; 12 984 of 1.5 million in 1-D CRD Ca II; 84 152 of 10.3 million in 1-D H + Ca II, 2 373 of them by bound B), whose sums over wavelength and angle stay positive. All 12 `run_tests.sh` cases then differ from the base (1.2e-2 to 1.8e-2 in I in the 4-iteration states, 1.4e-1 in 1-D H + Ca II at Lyman alpha) and the iteration path changes: converged to 1e-9, the spectra agree with the base to 2.3e-9 (1-D CRD Ca II, 28 iterations both) and 8.5e-9 (1-D H + Ca II, 46 iterations on the base, 49 with the limiter), so the fixed point is the same but the cost is not zero. A limiter acting at the level of the summed rate would need the sums of all wavelengths, which `addtoGamma` does not have. Not worth a change of every run's path for a failure that does not occur; the backstop above catches it if it does.
