title: One transfer driver for 1-D, 2-D and 3-D, with a table of geometry operations (results unchanged)

The 1-D, 2-D and 3-D programs each had their own `Formal()`, the formal solution at one wavelength. The three were the same driver and differed only in the call of the ray solver, the columns of the negative optical depth check and the quadrature it integrates χ with, where the emergent intensity is stored (`I[0]`, a row, a plane), and the background scattering iteration of 1-D (N-51, N-52). The driver is now one function in the library, `src/transfer/formal.c` (the former 1-D `Formal()`), and each geometry provides what differs through a table of operations, `geometryOps` (`src/transfer/geometryops.h`, `docs/LIBRARY.md` §3.4): the rays (μ and weight), the unpolarized and polarized ray solvers, the negative optical depth check, the emergent intensity, and for the background scattering iteration whether it is supported, whether non-positive solvers fall back to the linear one, the largest albedo and the row of the operator. `bgScatterSupported()` and `bgScatterLinearFallback()`, which the library called in the program, are now entries of the table.

The background scattering iteration (N-51) is part of the driver for every geometry; 2-D and 3-D still say they do not support it (N-68), so nothing changes there. `rhsphere` keeps its own formal solution through an entry of the table that replaces the driver, until Q-43 decides whether it joins.

`vproject`, `RII` and `Hydrostatic` stay functions that each program provides: the library's physics calls them, not the driver, and they move when the geometries are linked into one program (phase 5).

Part of Q-5. Results unchanged: `run_tests.py --exact` against the base, all geometries.
