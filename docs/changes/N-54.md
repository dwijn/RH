title: The zero of the height scale of a column-mass or tau500 atmosphere is found in ln tau_ref, and an atmosphere that does not reach tau_ref = 1 stops the run

For an atmosphere given in `COLUMN_MASS` or `TAU500`, `convertScales` (`rhf1d/multiatmos.c`, `rhsphere/readatmos.c`) builds the height (1-D) or radius (spherical) scale from 0 downward, then shifts it so that it is zero at tau_ref = 1. The shift came from `Linear(Ndep, tau_ref, height, 1, &unity, &h_zero)`: linear interpolation in tau_ref, and the end value when 1 lies outside the table. Both are wrong. tau_ref and height are related roughly exponentially, so the interpolation belongs in ln tau_ref. And an atmosphere that stops above tau_ref = 1 got the height of its last point as the zero, with no message: the scale was shifted to the bottom (measured: an atmosphere cut at tau_ref = 7.6e-6 got heights from 601.6 km at the top to 0 at the bottom).

**Fix.** New `heightAtUnityTau` (`linear.c`) interpolates linearly in ln tau_ref between the two bracketing points and ignores points with tau_ref <= 0 (the top point of a `TAU500` table can be 0 after unit conversion; ln 0 never enters). The wrapper `zeroPointAtUnityTau` stops with ERROR_LEVEL_2 and a message naming the routine, the atmosphere ID and the tau_ref range when tau_ref = 1 is not inside the range, and when tau_ref is not strictly increasing with depth index. Both programs call it. The message suggests a `GEOMETRIC` scale or a deeper atmosphere. The unit test (`test_interp`) checks the exactness for z = a + b ln tau_ref, nodes at exactly 1, a zero at the top, and the three return codes.

**What the shift affects.**
- rhf1d: only the height values written to `geometry.out`. The formal solvers and everything else use differences of heights, so the spectrum is unchanged.
- rhsphere: the shift of r matters. The rays are built from `geometry->Radius + r[k]` (`rhsphere/geometry.c`: impact parameters, path lengths, mu) and `formal.c` scales by `((r[0] + Radius)/Radius)^2`, so `Radius` (696 000 km in the shipped files) is the radius of the tau_ref = 1 surface and the zero of r must sit there. The emergent intensities change a little with the corrected zero (below).

**Shipped atmospheres.** Every shipped text atmosphere in `Atmos/` and `tests/atmos/` that is read by rhf1d or rhsphere uses a mass scale (`FALA_80`, `FALC_82`, `FALC_82_s`, `FALF_80`, `FALXCO_80`, `FALXCO_80_s`, and the test atmospheres `FALC_shock`, `FALC_v10`); none uses `TAU500`. `T5750_g4.5_p00_ext` has a height scale; the binary 2-D/3-D atmospheres are read by other programs. In all six FAL atmospheres tau_ref = 1 lies inside the table (tau_ref from about 1e-6 to 24), so the new error breaks none of them.

**Measured change of the zero (new minus old height, all points shift equally; tau_ref and column mass are identical).**

| atmosphere | shift (km) |
|---|---|
| `FALA_80` | +0.0745 |
| `FALC_82`, `FALC_82_s` | +0.0468 |
| `FALF_80` | +0.0725 |
| `FALXCO_80`, `FALXCO_80_s` | +0.0334 |

The shifts are small because the shipped tables are dense around tau_ref = 1 (a few km per point there). A coarse table would show more: the linear-in-tau interpolation error is of the order of the step times the curvature of tau(h) across a cell.

**Results that change.** Heights in `geometry.out` of 1-D runs (a uniform shift of at most 0.075 km in the shipped atmospheres). In spherical runs the same shift of r, hence a small change of the intensities: in the `run_tests.py` case `sph/small` (`FALC_82_s`) the largest relative difference of I is 1.5e-8 (at 22.79 nm), against a default tolerance of 1e-9; it passes at `--tol 1e-7`. This is the `changes-results` case. All 1-D, 2-D and 3-D cases are unchanged (1-D: I identical, except `1d/twoactive` at 8.7e-13; the 1-D spectrum does not depend on the height zero).
