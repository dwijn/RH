title: The exponential Stokes step of B-55 in closed form: half the time of a 2-D FULL_STOKES run

B-55 (#371) made the polarized DELO steps fall back to an exact exponential step where max |K'| Δτ is large. Each half step evaluates e^A and φ₁…φ₄ of A = −Δτ (1 + K′), a 4 × 4 matrix, by a Taylor series and squarings (`exp_phi4`). That doubled the cost of a 2-D `FULL_STOKES` run: on the `run_tests` case (FALC_03x82 with the field of `make_field.py`, Ca II, one thread, four iterations) `exp_phi4` took 60 % of the time, 3.8 µs per call over 10.3 M calls, 1.85 s of the 3.2 s per formal solution (1.36 s before B-55, measured on the commit before #371 for the formal solutions it completed before diverging). The fallback is taken in 32 % of the Bézier steps there. Most of its calls are not thick steps but steps with strong magneto-optical terms: B = −Δτ K′ has eigenvalues ±a (dichroism) and ±i b (Faraday rotation), and in the line wings b is large (up to 10⁴ rad in a step) while a is small (median about 10⁻²·⁵). The squarings follow the rotation, about log₂ b of them (4.4 on average, up to 13).

K′ has the structure of a generator of the Lorentz group (η symmetric in the first row and column, ρ antisymmetric in the 3 × 3 block), so a² and −b² are the roots of s² − p s − q², p = Δτ² (η² − ρ²), q = Δτ² η·ρ, and B satisfies (B² − a²)(B² + b²) = 0. Any function of A is then a cubic in B: f(A) = α₀ + β₀ B + α₁ B² + β₁ B³, with the coefficients from the even and odd parts of g(x) = f(−Δτ + x) on the pairs ±a and ±i b. `exp_step_spectral` computes them and applies the cubic to the five vectors of the step (I_uw and the Hermite coefficients of S), so no matrix function is formed. The even and odd parts are computed without dividing by a small a or b (`exp_pair`): from e^z cosh r and e^z sinh(r)/r with the recurrence φ_{k+1}(w) = (φ_k(w) − 1/k!)/w written for the pair, or from the power series where Δτ < 3, so the common case of small a and large b loses nothing. Where K′ is close to degenerate (|η| = |ρ| with η ⊥ ρ, a² + b² below 1 % of Δτ² (η² + ρ²)), or not of that form, `exp_phi4` is used as before, and so are the thick steps, which already had a cheap path.

Accuracy, against the same step in long double (prototype, 300 000 random steps binned by a and b): the closed form is as accurate as the Taylor series, 10⁻¹⁶ to 10⁻¹³ relative up to b ≈ 10, growing as b ε beyond, where both are limited by the rounding of the phase b (at b ≈ 10⁵: 2 × 10⁻⁹ against 4 × 10⁻¹⁰ for the Taylor series). `test_stokes_step` compares the two paths (`Stokes_Exp_const_step` with `closed_form` FALSE, for the test) on a grid of a from 10⁻⁶ to 10² and b from 10⁻⁶ to 10⁴ and on random K′: within 7 % of a bound of 10⁻¹³ max(10, b); it checks that the closed form is taken (at all 99 grid points) and that a degenerate K′ takes the Taylor path.

Measured (sundog2, one thread, three runs each, interleaved): the 2-D `FULL_STOKES` case above 32.9 s against 64.4 s on modernize ae1db1e, the same 21 formal solutions, the same convergence history, emergent spectra equal to 6 × 10⁻¹³ (I, Q, U, V). Per formal solution 1.57 s, against 1.36 s before B-55. A prototype step costs 0.9 µs against 5.5 µs (random steps, 84 % on the closed form).

Results change at rounding level wherever the exponential fallback is taken (polarized multi-D runs, and 1-D `FULL_STOKES` where max |K′| Δτ > 1). `run_tests.sh` in tolerance mode against modernize ae1db1e: passed 38, failed 0; the cases without polarization are byte-identical, the polarized ones (1-D, 2-D and 3-D `FIELD_FREE`, `FULL_STOKES`, `lcbezier`) differ by at most 9.2 × 10⁻¹³ (1-D `FULL_STOKES`, V); peak memory unchanged.
