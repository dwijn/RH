title: An Ng step that makes a population non-positive or non-finite is rejected, and the plain step kept

`2d_pert_h6_ca_mg` stopped with SIGFPE at iteration 6 since B-40 (#257). The violent iteration 5 was Ng's first extrapolation (`NG_DELAY = 6` in the case), not the MALI iteration: at that point every iteration still changed H by about 100 %, the extrapolation made one H population negative (largest change 424, 83 before B-40), and the next iteration had negative radiative rates, a negative opacity and an `exp` overflow in `Bezier3_coeffs`. Without Ng the case is stable: plain G converges in 149 iterations, Anderson in 169. `Accelerate()` now checks that the extrapolation keeps the sign of the unaccelerated solution g: no value below 0 where g ≥ 0, none at 0 where g > 0, and none non-finite where g is finite (with the floating-point traps of `SetFPEtraps` an overflow still traps before the check; the non-finite test matters on platforms without them). Otherwise the step is rejected and g restored, also in the Ng history. The check covers every user of `Accelerate()`: populations, molecules, PRD ρ, chemical equilibrium, hydrostatic equilibrium and the source-function iterations of the 1-D and 2-D scattering solvers. Where the caller prints a log line for the step (populations, molecules, PRD ρ, the scattering iterations), it says `(Ng step rejected: N values not positive or not finite; plain step kept)` instead of `(accelerated)`; chemical and hydrostatic equilibrium print none, as before. The whole step of an atom (or line) is rejected, not the offending values: a damped step that keeps positivity would keep more of the acceleration, and is left for N-2. With it, `2d_pert_h6_ca_mg` converges under Ng in 90 iterations (one step rejected). Runs in which no extrapolation goes non-positive are unchanged (`run_tests.sh --exact` byte-identical). The unit test `test_ng` checks acceptance, rejection and the history on linear contractions where Ng of order 1 is exact. This is the positivity part of N-2; Ng still has no residual or conditioning check.
