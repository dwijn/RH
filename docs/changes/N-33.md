title: An Ng step that makes a population non-positive or non-finite is rejected, and the plain step kept

`2d_pert_h6_ca_mg` stopped with SIGFPE at iteration 6 since B-40 (#257). The violent iteration 5 was Ng's first extrapolation (`NG_DELAY = 6` in the case), not the MALI iteration: at that point every iteration still changed H by about 100 %, the extrapolation made one H population negative (largest change 424, 83 before B-40), and the next iteration had negative radiative rates, a negative opacity and an `exp` overflow in `Bezier3_coeffs`. Without Ng the case is stable: plain G converges in 149 iterations, Anderson in 169. `Accelerate()` now checks the extrapolated vector: if a value is not finite, or not positive where the unaccelerated solution is positive, the step is rejected, the unaccelerated solution is restored (also in the Ng history), and the log line says `(Ng step rejected: N values not positive or not finite; plain step kept)` instead of `(accelerated)`. The check covers every user of `Accelerate()`: populations, molecules, PRD ρ, chemical equilibrium, hydrostatic equilibrium and the source-function iterations of the 1-D and 2-D scattering solvers. With it, `2d_pert_h6_ca_mg` converges under Ng in 90 iterations (one step rejected). Runs in which no extrapolation goes non-positive are unchanged (`run_tests.sh --exact` byte-identical). The unit test `test_ng` checks acceptance, rejection and the history on linear contractions where Ng of order 1 is exact. This is the positivity part of N-2; Ng still has no residual or conditioning check.
