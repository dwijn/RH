title: Convergence report of the G solvers: rho and ne residuals, estimated contraction and error; what ITER_LIMIT guarantees

`ITER_LIMIT` with the G solvers (ANDERSON, FIXED_POINT, NEWTON_KRYLOV) tests the largest over the active atoms and molecules of max |G(x) − x| / |x|, a relative fixed-point residual and not the error: the error is about r / (1 − λ) with λ the contraction of G, close to 1 when convergence is slow. The state also holds the PRD ρ blocks and ne (`SOLVE_NE = ITERATION`), which are not in the test. This change reports more and documents the test; the test itself, and therefore every result, is unchanged.

The per-iteration lines now also carry the residual (same measure, on x and G(x)) of the ρ blocks, one line per atom with PRD lines (` rho CA: delta = ...`), and of ne when it is in the state (` ne: delta = ...`). At the end of a run of a G solver, converged or not, one line follows the unchanged ` -- Converged in ...` / `NOT CONVERGED` / `Stopped on request` message: ` -- Estimated contraction 0.959 per iteration (last 10 iterations); the error of the populations may be up to about 2.4E-03 (residual / (1 - contraction))`. λ = (r_n / r_{n−m})^{1/m} is the geometric mean of the ratio of successive residuals over the last m ≤ 10 accepted iterations (a point Anderson rejects is not counted; Newton counts base points); the line says `no estimate` for fewer than three iterations or when λ ≥ 1. `SOLVER = NG` is unchanged (no such line). The log parsers (`tests/analyze_run.py`, which reads ` ID, delta = ` lines between ` -- Iteration` markers, the e2e scripts and `run_stress.sh`) match none of the new lines.

Documentation: the example `keyword.input` files (rhf1d, rhsc2d, rhsc3d, rhsphere) say next to `ITER_LIMIT` what it tests and what it does not guarantee; `docs/SOLVER.md` §2.4 states it, with the format of the new lines.

How good is the estimate (`e2e_convergence_report`; Ca II with two angle-averaged PRD lines on FAL-C, ITER_LIMIT 1e-4 against a run of ANDERSON to 1e-9, 162 iterations; error = max relative difference of the Ca II populations to that reference, over all levels and depths): FIXED_POINT without Ng, where the residuals follow the contraction of G, 84 iterations, λ = 0.959, estimated 2.4×10⁻³, actual 5.2×10⁻³ (ratio 0.47); over ITER_LIMIT 10⁻², 10⁻³, 3×10⁻⁴, 10⁻⁴, 3×10⁻⁵, 10⁻⁵ the ratio is 0.67, 0.80, 0.47, 0.47, 0.75, 0.98 (λ rises from 0.84 to 0.99). ANDERSON: λ = 0.79, estimated 2.0×10⁻⁴, actual 3.0×10⁻³ (ratio 0.07; over the same limits and 10⁻⁶ the ratio is 0.28, 0.54, 0.63, 0.07, 0.05, 0.66, 0.05); NEWTON_KRYLOV: λ = 0.45, estimated 1.1×10⁻⁴, actual 1.4×10⁻³ (ratio 0.08; same limits: 0.16, 0.27, 0.15, 0.08, 0.43, 0.15, 0.07). So the estimate is a fair guide for the plain iteration and an underestimate, by up to about 20 times, for the accelerated solvers: their residual falls at the rate of the accelerated iteration (and Anderson's is not monotone, so the last residual can be a dip), while the error is set by the slow modes of G that the acceleration removes from the residual first. The line says "may be up to about", the documentation says it is an indication and not a bound, and a caller that needs a defined accuracy should compare with a run at a smaller `ITER_LIMIT`. `e2e_convergence_report` checks FIXED_POINT to a factor of 3 and the other two only to a factor of 30, and prints the numbers.

Results do not change: `run_tests.sh --exact` against modernize f494188.
