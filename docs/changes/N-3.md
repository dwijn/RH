title: ERROR_LIMIT: ANDERSON and NEWTON_KRYLOV can stop on the estimated error instead of the residual

`ITER_LIMIT` tests the relative fixed-point residual of the populations, max |G(x) − x| / |G(x)|, not their error. The error is roughly the residual divided by (1 − λ), with λ the contraction of G. The acceleration of `ANDERSON` and `NEWTON_KRYLOV` lowers the residual much faster than the error. PRD ρ, ne and J are in the state but not in the test.

Measured against converged runs (residual 10⁻⁹) over 21 1-D cases (all `run_stress.py` convergence cases but `collrel`, plus three Bifrost columns), with `ANDERSON` stopped at `ITER_LIMIT` = 10⁻² … 10⁻⁵:
- The population error at the stop is a median of 5 times `ITER_LIMIT`, and up to 47–100 times. `1d_t5750_h6` is 93 % off at 10⁻²; at 10⁻⁴ it is 1 % off.
- `NEWTON_KRYLOV` is similar (median 5–7, max about 100); 2-D and 3-D `ANDERSON` reach 14–63.
- J at background-scattering wavelengths converges only with the main iteration (`N_MAX_SCATTER = 2` in the shipped keyword files). With the populations to 9×10⁻⁵, the emergent intensity of `1d_falc_fe33` at 122 nm is 6.5×10⁻³ off. On the FAL-C Ca II case of the `convergence_report` test it is 55 % off at 125 nm with the populations stopped at 10⁻⁴.

**The new keyword.** `ERROR_LIMIT` (optional; `ANDERSON` and `NEWTON_KRYLOV` only) stops the run when the estimated error of the output G(x) is below it. The error is the largest relative error over the populations, PRD ρ, ne (when in the state) and J.
- **When an estimate is made.** Once the residual of all of these is below a target, which starts at `ERROR_LIMIT`, six plain G steps are taken from G(x). Minimal polynomial extrapolation (new `mpe.c`) of their logarithms estimates the fixed point.
- **If the estimate is too large,** the target drops to 0.8 r·`ERROR_LIMIT`/e and the solver continues from where it was.
- **Before a stop,** G(x) is evaluated once more, so that the rates and intensities written are those of the output.
- **With both keywords** both tests must pass. With `ERROR_LIMIT` alone, `ITER_LIMIT` takes its value; it also limits the background-scattering iterations. These two solvers need at least one of the two keywords.
- **Other solvers** still need `ITER_LIMIT` and stop at input on `ERROR_LIMIT`.
- **Without `ERROR_LIMIT`** nothing changes: `run_tests.py --exact` is byte-identical.
- **A run that reaches `N_MAX_ITER`** reports the estimated error of its output.
- **The log** adds `J: delta` per iteration, a line per estimate, and the number of estimates and their G evaluations.

Why MPE: the residual history of the accelerated solvers says nothing about λ. Its estimate r/(1 − λ) was 0.07–1.9 times the actual error, and the contraction read off Anderson's own secant pairs 0.4–8.6 times. r/(1 − λ) from a few plain steps works until plain G is not a contraction at the solution: on `1d_shock_h6crd_ne` its residual grows 1.85 times per step while Anderson converges. MPE extrapolates through that, and gives 0.6–1.4 times the actual error with six steps. With five it drops to 0.47 in 2-D and 3-D.

Most runs make two estimates, about 13 G evaluations in all. The first, at a residual of `ERROR_LIMIT`, usually fails, because the error is several times the residual. Some cases converge slowly in J at background-scattering wavelengths, which `ITER_LIMIT` never waited for. They cost more: the `convergence_report` case takes 260 iterations at `ERROR_LIMIT` = 10⁻⁴ instead of 33 at `ITER_LIMIT` = 10⁻⁴. About 40 % of that is the Anderson safeguard rejecting every other extrapolated point and restarting the history (152 iterations with `ANDERSON_SAFEGUARD` = 2 or 0).

New unit test `test_mpe`: MPE is exact on a linear iteration whose minimal polynomial has degree 5, including an eigenvalue of 1.85; it drops dependent columns; it reports that no limit exists when λ = 1. The `convergence_report` e2e test checks:
- the `ERROR_LIMIT` log lines with both solvers;
- an error within 1.5×10⁻⁴ of the reference at `ERROR_LIMIT` = 10⁻⁴;
- the message with both keywords set;
- the report at `N_MAX_ITER`;
- the five keyword checks at input;
- that runs without `ERROR_LIMIT` print none of the new lines.
