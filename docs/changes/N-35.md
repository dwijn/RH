title: H-minus bound-free cross-section from McLaughlin et al. (2017): fits outside a table read at run time (Atoms/Hminus_bf.ecsv)

`Hminus_bf` (`hydrogen.c`) used the table of Geltman (1962), 34 nodes from 0 to 1641.9 nm with a natural cubic spline. It now uses the merged and scaled single-detachment cross-section of McLaughlin, Stancil, Sadeghpour & Forrey (2017, J. Phys. B 50, 114001, supplementary data; doi:10.1088/1361-6455/aa6c1f), in three parts:

- between the first and last wavelength of a table read at run time: linear interpolation in the table. The table of the distribution, `Atoms/Hminus_bf.ecsv`, has 60 non-uniform knots from 80 to 125 nm (0.1 nm apart through the n = 2 shape and Feshbach resonances at 112-114 nm and the dip at 97.9 nm, up to 5.7 nm apart where the cross-section is smooth). The knots are means of the source over 0.1 nm bins, placed so that linear interpolation stays within 1 % of the bin means, with the bins the source leaves empty (13, mostly at the n >= 2 thresholds) bridged linearly;
- below the table: sigma = A E^p (1 + (Ec/E)^q)^-s Mb (E in eV), with A = 71425.67, p = -3.295012 the power law the source follows from 50 to 1000 eV (to 6e-4), and Ec = 39.105 eV, q = 4.5305, s = 0.15471 fitted below 85 nm. Within 1.9 % of the source from 30 to 80 nm and 1-2 % below 20 nm; up to 4 % at 20-25 nm, where the source itself steps by 4 % at 50 eV (its switch from R-matrix to the power law);
- above the table up to the threshold: the form of John (1988, A&A 193, 189), sigma = lambda^3 x^(3/2) sum_n C_n x^(n/2) Mb (lambda in micron, x = 1/lambda - 1/lambda0), refitted to the source above 120 nm with lambda0 = 1643.908 nm (E0 = 0.754204 eV, the threshold of the source; John's coefficients and Geltman's table use 1641.9 nm). Within 0.7 % of the source from 125 to 1500 nm (John's coefficients: 2 %), 3 % in the last 2 % before threshold.

The fits hold only outside 85-120 nm, so a table must cover that range; it may be replaced by another (keyword `HMINUS_BF_DATA`, KEYWORD_DEFAULT, default `../../Atoms/Hminus_bf.ecsv`, like `KURUCZ_PF_DATA`), and the fits then take over at its ends. The table is ECSV (astropy's Enhanced Character Separated Values, APE 6); RH reads the subset the generator writes: space-delimited, the columns `lambda` [nm] and `sigma` [Mbarn] in this order (checked against the header), lambda strictly increasing, sigma >= 0. A file that is missing, of another form, or does not cover 85-120 nm below threshold stops the run with a message; when a join between table and fit steps by more than 2 % RH warns. The ends of the table of the distribution take the values of the fits, so its joins are continuous (to 1e-6, the rounding of the knots). The table is read once in `hydrogenPrepare`, before threading, and kept across `hydrogenCleanup`, as the Geltman spline was.

`docs/opacity/hminus_bf_table.py` makes the table and prints the constants of `hydrogen.c` from the source file, which is not part of RH (the authors retain its copyright; IOP holds a non-exclusive licence and claims none in research data): RH ships the derived table with the citation. The exploration behind the choices (forms tried for the short-wavelength fit, bin widths, knot tolerance) is in the notebook of the evidence, `/hao/solar1/dwijn/claude/RH/evidence/n35-mcl/` (README), with the scripts and logs of the measurements below.

Measured (`run_tests.sh` in tolerance mode, candidate against a build of 42667f4): all 12 comparisons with the baseline differ, by 2.0-2.1 % in I at 387-390 nm in every case except `1d/twoactive` and `sph/small` (5.0-5.1 % at 220.7 nm, the cases with the 200-300 nm continuum); Q, U, V by at most 1.6e-4 (relative to the peak). The 26 other checks pass: every case at 3 and 8 threads against its own 1-thread result, the memory case (113 MB against 115 MB) and the Kurucz list with a leading blank line. The continuum at mu = 0.953 on FAL-C (`1d/twoactive`) changes by -1.9 % at 205 nm, -4.0 % at 249 nm, -3.1 % at 302 nm, -1.8 % at 350 nm, -1.9 % at 400 nm, -1.2 % at 500 nm, -0.7 % at 595 nm and -0.1 to -0.5 % at 0.8-1.9 micron, and by less than 5e-5 below 150 nm, where H-minus bound-free is at most 6e-4 of the background opacity (N-35 evidence). The cross-section of Geltman is 0.86, 0.89, 0.93, 0.97 and 0.98 of the new one at 150, 200, 300, 400 and 500 nm, 1.000 +- 0.008 at 0.8-1.4 micron, and 1.25 at 1600 nm (its threshold at 1641.9 nm instead of 1643.9 nm). How close the approximation is: the same FAL-C continuum (all atoms passive, 12000 wavelengths from 50 to 2500 nm, 0.005 nm apart at 80-125 nm, mu = 0.953) computed with the default table and with the whole source as the table (`HMINUS_BF_DATA` = the 35908 points of the source, 0.012-1643.7 nm, so that the fits cover only the outer slivers; RH warns that its ends do not join the fits) agrees to 0.19 % everywhere: 0.12 % at 125-300 nm and 0.19 % at 300-1000 nm (the 0.7 % residual of the John form, scaled by the share of H-minus bound-free), 0.034 % at 80-125 nm (single points in the Feshbach resonance at 113.4 nm, which the table averages), less than 1e-5 below 80 nm. Geltman differs from the full table by up to 5.9 % (207 nm). Label: changes-results.

Tests: `tests/unit/test_hminus_bf.c` (43 checks): the table of the distribution reads, both joins are continuous, the fits match the generator to 1e-9, a table knot is returned exactly, sigma = 0 at and beyond threshold and for lambda <= 0, a synthetic table interpolates linearly with the fits outside it, and nine malformed tables (no ECSV line, wrong unit, comma delimiter, columns swapped, three columns, lambda not increasing, sigma < 0, table ending above 85 or below 120 nm) and a missing file are refused with a message while the table read before stays in use.
