title: Hybrid PRD: `PRDH_LIMIT_MEM` computes the weights of the tables, with one code path, and both get the ends of the grid right

With `PRD_ANGLE_DEP = PRD_ANGLE_APPROX` the formal solution of each wavelength adds its intensity, shifted to the gas frame, to the gas-frame mean intensity Jgas of neighbouring wavelengths, and `Opacity` interpolates ρ to the Doppler-shifted wavelength of each ray. The weights are tabulated once (P-12), or, with `PRDH_LIMIT_MEM = TRUE`, the option for grids whose tables do not fit (about 32 B per point, wavelength, ray and direction: 19 GB for a 1-D column of 496 points and 118 501 wavelengths, ~770 B per point and wavelength in 3-D with A4), computed when needed. That second path had code of its own, repeated in the three `formal.c`: every formal solution of every ray scanned the whole wavelength grid at every location, which is the O(Nspect² Nrays Nspace) setup of P-12 again in every sweep, and added each point under its own lock; `Opacity` copied the line's ρ at each location and interpolated it with `Linear`.

`prdh.c` now holds the weights: `prdhBracket` (the bracketing search of P-12) and `prdhWeight` for Jgas, `prdhRhoWeights` for ρ, `prdhShift` for the Doppler factor, and `initPRDhTables`, which builds both tables from them (moved from `initSolution`). `addJgasRay` takes the weights of a ray from the table or from `prdhBracket` and adds them in the same order, with the per-row buffering and locking of T-4 in both cases; the three `PRDH_LIMIT_MEM` branches of the formal solutions are gone. `Opacity` interpolates ρ with the tabulated weights or with `prdhRhoWeights`, by the same expression, and no longer needs a scratch array (P-16). So `PRDH_LIMIT_MEM` changes memory and time, not results.

The ends of the grid (B-56, also upstream): there a neighbour of a wavelength is the wavelength itself, and the points beyond are taken with weight 1. The table took the points at or above the gas-frame wavelength at the upper end, so that where the line-of-sight velocity is exactly 0 the last wavelength added its intensity to its own Jgas twice (once more through the bracket below it); `PRDH_LIMIT_MEM` took the points below it at the lower end, so that the first wavelength added nothing to its own. Both now take the points at or below at the lower end and above at the upper end. This changes Jgas only at the two ends of the grid, at locations at rest, when that wavelength is solved angle by angle, which matters only if a PRD line reaches the end of the grid.

Tests: `test_prdh` checks that `prdhBracket` selects the points of a scan of the whole grid (random grids and shifts), that the weights are a partition of unity (each grid point gets a total weight of 1, also at the ends; with no shift each wavelength gives weight 1 to itself only), that `addJgasRay` gives the same Jgas to the bit from the table and with `PRDH_LIMIT_MEM`, with Jgas = J at locations at rest, and that `prdhRhoWeights` interpolates linearly and is constant beyond the line's grid; it fails with either old rule at the ends. `e2e_prd_hybrid` adds a moving atmosphere (`tests/atmos/FALC_v10.atmos`, 29 depths at rest, three rays): Jgas = J at the depths at rest at every wavelength, with the table and with `PRDH_LIMIT_MEM`, and the `PRDH_LIMIT_MEM` run equals the table run to the bit. Before this change the first check fails with the table (Jgas = 2 J at the last wavelength) and so does the second; the old `PRDH_LIMIT_MEM` rule at the lower end does not show there, because the first wavelength of that grid has no line and is solved without angles.

Results: with the tables unchanged to the bit unless a location is exactly at rest (1-D Bifrost column, 77 points, Fe I+II and Mg active, 118 501 wavelengths, three iterations on one thread: populations, J, Jgas and flux identical to P-16); with `PRDH_LIMIT_MEM` identical to the table run. On that column one iteration on 8 threads takes 45 s and 7.4 GB with the tables, 36 s and 3.6 GB with `PRDH_LIMIT_MEM`; over three iterations on one thread the iterations take 30 % longer without the tables (187 s against 144 s).
