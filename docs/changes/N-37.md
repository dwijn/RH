title: 2-D short characteristics interpolate between grid points with a monotonic cubic Bézier instead of the switched quadratic

The 2-D short characteristics interpolated I, S and χ at the upwind and downwind points with `Quadr`, a quadratic through three grid points that switched to the linear interpolation of the nearest pair where the parabola overshot. That switch is a jump in the interpolated value as a function of the data, so the iteration map is discontinuous and need not have a fixed point: on `2d_pert_h6_ca_mg` every solver, plain G included, ended in a cycle (all CRD: H with period 5 at 10⁻³; with PRD a floor of 2–5×10⁻⁵). `Interp_2D` (static inline in `rhsc2d/geometry.h`) replaces it: a cubic Bézier between the two grid points that bound the crossing, with the derivatives of Steffen (1990) at both ends, the same construction the Bézier formal solvers use along the ray. Its control points stay between the two end values, so the result never leaves their range and is continuous in the data. Where the neighbour beyond an end is missing (non-periodic boundaries, and on a vertical crossing the layer the sweep has not computed yet) the end derivative is Steffen's one-sided one, and with neither neighbour the secant. The stencil stores 1/h, the two Steffen weights and h/3, so the interpolation does no division. The new keyword `INTERPOLATE_2D` selects `BEZIER_2D` (default) or `LINEAR_2D`; the quadratic, its triplet selection and `SC_QUADRATIC` are removed, and `StokesK_2D` loses its `monotonic` argument. Convergence: all CRD, the iteration reaches `ITER_LIMIT` 10⁻⁴ on the perturbed FAL-C at 32, 64 and 128 columns in 248, 259 and 240 iterations (linear 234, 234, 224), where the old scheme never does (it cycles at 1–3×10⁻³); with PRD, from scratch, it passes 10⁻⁴ at iteration 304 (old 272) and keeps falling, 2.3×10⁻⁵ at 400, until a separate Mg II period-2 cycle with PRD sets in at 4×10⁻⁶ (N-38). Accuracy: in a horizontal resolution study (same domain at 32, 64 and 128 columns, all CRD) the Bézier and old schemes agree at 128 columns (Mg median 5×10⁻⁵, 99th percentile 7 %), and against that reference, at the points where any scheme errs by more than 5 %, Bézier has the smallest error at 77–88 % of them (median error 0.07–0.09 against 0.12–0.17 for linear and the old scheme); the old scheme and linear let more ionizing radiation into the cool columns of the upper layers (Mg III fraction at one point 0.072 Bézier, 0.150 linear, 0.189 old at 32 columns, 0.018–0.023 for all at 128). That model is not resolved horizontally even at 128 columns. Results change: `2d/small` differs by up to 2.2×10⁻² in I (115 nm). Cost: 25 % more time per iteration than the old quadratic on `2d/small` inputs (6.31 against 5.04 s on one thread; linear 4.67 s). The unit test `test_formal_2d` checks `Interp_2D` (exact for linear and quadratic data, inside the range of its end values, continuous in the data), every short- and long-characteristic stencil of `fillMesh` on an uneven periodic mesh (exact for linear fields, and quadratic ones where all four points are present), and `LINEAR_2D`. The polarized solver still interpolates component by component with a limiter, so N-26 remains; 3-D is not changed.
