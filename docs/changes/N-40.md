title: A negative total opacity that would overflow the formal solution stops the run with a message, or rejects the trial point

Where the total opacity is negative (a strong inverted line, SOLVER.md trap 5), the intensity grows as exp(−τ) across the run of negative optical depth, and the formal solvers overflow once that run is deeper than about −709: the run died with SIGFPE, or without floating-point traps carried inf and NaN into J and the populations, with nothing saying where. Nothing caught it before: the line test of Anderson's guard did not keep the total opacity positive (S-13, S-14).

Each `Formal()` (1-D, 2-D, 3-D, spherical) now checks the opacity of every wavelength and ray before solving (`negtau.c`). It takes the optical depth of each depth interval along the ray and finds the most negative sum over a contiguous stretch of intervals (a minimum-subarray sum: the intensity grows by exp(−Δτ) per interval, so zero or slightly positive intervals between negative ones do not stop the growth). In 2-D and 3-D each column is checked on its own, scaled by 1/μz; a bound that took every interval in its most negative column could add up patches no ray crosses and stop a run that would finish, while the threshold leaves more than a factor ten before an overflow for a ray that crosses columns. A spherical ray is checked along its own path. Angle-independent wavelengths are checked once, with the most slanted ray. A stretch below −50 (`NEG_TAU_LIMIT`; overflow is at about −709, and the S-5 set never goes below −0.1) skips the rest of that wavelength, before anything can overflow, and `solveSpectrum` skips the wavelengths left. Then:

- Outside a trial point (the initial lambda iteration, plain and Ng steps, the final formal solution, every solver), `solveSpectrum` stops the run with a message naming the wavelength, the ray (and direction where the opacity depends on it), the depth points that bound the stretch, the column in 2-D and 3-D, and the optical depth, e.g. "negative total opacity at lambda = 392.906561 nm (wavelength 40), ray 0: optical depth -120 between depth points 0 and 2, column 2 (limit -50)".
- At an extrapolated Anderson point, `G_eval` returns FALSE and the point is rejected like one that fails the safeguard: the history restarts and the next point is G at the last accepted point. The log says so for each one and counts them at the end. In Newton's line search the trial counts as a guard halving ("negative opacity"); a finite-difference probe of the Jacobian that meets it gives a zero product and is counted.

A real case: Newton–Krylov on `shock_h6` overflowed in `Bezier3_coeffs` with SIGFPE (after 29 steps without the line test of the guard, S-14, and after 139 with it). With the detector it finds the negative opacity in 10 trial points, halves λ instead, and runs to its budget (3000 G evaluations; Newton–Krylov does not converge `shock_h6`).

Not covered: a total opacity of exactly zero, which still divides by zero in S = η/χ.

Where no total opacity is negative the check is one pass over χ per ray; where it is negative but the runs are shallow (the S-5 set: −0.094 at most, on `falc_mgi66`), it changes nothing. `run_tests.sh --exact` against the S-14 build: 34 of 34 cases bit-identical. Unit test `test_negtau`: the minimum-subarray sum (including stretches across zero and positive intervals), the threshold, the per-column check (patches in different columns do not add up), the message. The two paths and Newton's were checked with a scratch hook that fakes a detection at one wavelength (not committed): the stop in 1-D, 2-D and spherical geometry, and Anderson and Newton–Krylov rejecting every trial point and every probe and finishing normally. Code review (medium) before the PR: its findings are in the second commit.
